<p class="research-lede">The universe does not fit on a laptop. Frontier-E, one of the cosmology simulations IDEAS works with, follows gravity, gas and the formation of galaxies across a volume of space the size of a sky survey, and it fills multiple petabytes across hundreds of snapshots. A single timestep of a modern simulation can run to terabytes. The structures that matter are in there somewhere. But scientists can explore only a sliver of that data interactively, because the tools they use to look were built for a smaller world.</p>

IDEAS, the Intelligent Data Exploration Assistant for Science, sets out to change that. The idea is simple to state and hard to achieve. If scientific data can be turned into compact models that keep what matters, those models can be rendered directly, questioned in plain language, and connected to the supercomputers and instruments where science happens. The goal is an assistant that helps a scientist explore a petabyte the way they explore a photograph: by looking, asking, and following their curiosity.

The work unfolds across four connected Objectives, each building on the one before. Every Objective answers a priority research direction identified by the DOE Visualization for Scientific Discovery, Decision-Making, and Communication Workshop.

<!-- objectives -->

Represent, render, converse, deploy: each Objective makes the next one possible. Together they are a bet that the hardest bottleneck in data-intensive science now sits between the data and the human mind, and that it can be narrowed. The [Use Cases]({{ '/use-cases/' | relative_url }}) show where this work meets real science, in cosmology, turbulence and X-ray science. The [Publications]({{ '/publications/' | relative_url }}) and [Software]({{ '/software/' | relative_url }}) pages are where results become tools that anyone can use.
